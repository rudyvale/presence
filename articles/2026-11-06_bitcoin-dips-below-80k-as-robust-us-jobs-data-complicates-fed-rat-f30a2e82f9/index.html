Bitcoin Dips Below $80k as Robust US Jobs Data Complicates Fed Rate Cut Hopes

Presence Editorial · November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Bitcoin has dropped beneath $80,000 following US jobless claims data that exceeded expectations and indicated layoffs stayed at historically low levels, which brings new inquiries regarding the Federal Reserve's upcoming interest rate choice. Bitcoin dropped beneath $80,000 following a brief period where it traded above $80,500, as purchasers found it difficult to sustain the rebound even though the cryptocurrency stayed higher over the last 24 hours.

US initial jobless claims dropped to 189,000, which was below the expected figure of 196,000, reaching the lowest level since 1969 and indicating that employers continue to be hesitant about cutting jobs. Data from the labor market that exceeded expectations might make it harder to anticipate additional Federal Reserve interest rate reductions, potentially exerting downward pressure on risk assets like Bitcoin.

Strong US jobless claims data complicates Fed rate cut expectations

US spot Bitcoin ETFs registered $74.6 million in net inflows on November 4, bringing an end to three consecutive sessions of withdrawals, even as flows across major funds stayed mixed. According to a November 5 US News report, initial unemployment claims fell by 6,000 to 189,000 for the week concluding on October 31, versus market expectations of 196,000. The prior week's figure was adjusted to 195,000.

The most recent data point corresponded to the minimum recorded since mid-July, a date that marked the lowest occurrence since 1969, indicating that American employers continue to hesitate about dismissing employees even amid economic uncertainty. At the time of writing, Bitcoin was trading near $79,356, reflecting a 1.3% gain over the preceding 24 hours based on CoinGecko data. Prior to this, the cryptocurrency had risen above $80,500 before its momentum faded and it fell back below the $80,000 threshold.

Bitcoin's recent pullback occurred after it recovered from a downturn linked to the US midterm elections, during which the cryptocurrency dipped toward $75,600 before buyers stepped back in. Robust data on US jobless claims is now complicating expectations for Federal Reserve interest rate cuts. The surprisingly low count of unemployment claims indicates that the US labor market remains resilient, even as companies stay hesitant to grow their workforces.

The Associated Press reports that weekly claims have stayed at or below 200,000 for eight consecutive weeks, with the four-week moving average dropping to 194,750. Nevertheless, the employment landscape presents mixed signals; continuing jobless claims, which track the number of Americans collecting unemployment benefits, increased by 23,000 to reach 1.718 million, per Reuters.

For eight consecutive weeks, weekly unemployment benefit filings have remained at or below 200,000, with the four-week average declining to 194,750. In contrast, claims for continuing benefits climbed by 23,000 to total 1.718 million. This divergence indicates that while job losses are minimal, individuals who have been unemployed are experiencing extended periods before securing new positions.

A robust employment landscape allows authorities to maintain higher interest rates while monitoring inflation trends, which may lessen the immediate need for monetary stimulus. This scenario might negatively impact speculative investments like digital currencies, especially if market participants anticipate a reduction in future rate adjustments.

During its October meeting, the Federal Reserve maintained its benchmark interest rate within the 3.75% to 4% range, leaving investors to look toward upcoming employment reports for signals regarding the central bank's next move. Following a turbulent week where institutional fund flows and the US midterm elections triggered significant price volatility, Bitcoin experienced another downturn.

On November 4, US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds saw net inflows of $74.6 million, marking an end to three straight sessions characterized by withdrawals. While Fidelity's FBTC drew in $236.7 million and BlackRock's IBIT saw outflows totaling $201.2 million, the data indicates that institutional demand has remained uneven even as the broader market posted a positive result.

Bitcoin slips below $80k as robust jobs data raises doubts on rate cuts

Bitcoin's failure to sustain trading above the $80,000 threshold has shifted market attention to the $79,000 to $79,500 zone as the immediate support area, where a sustained breach could reveal the $78,000 level. This price recovery was underpinned by a wave of short liquidations that cleared approximately $378 million in bearish cryptocurrency positions within an hour.

On the upside, buyers must reclaim $80,000 and push through the $80,500 to $80,800 region to regain momentum. The broader market context is shaped by the upcoming October employment report scheduled for release on November 6, which economists surveyed by FactSet expect will show employers adding approximately 65,000 jobs while keeping the unemployment rate at 4.2%. This data point remains prospective and could further complicate Federal Reserve rate cut hopes as investors await confirmation of labor market resilience.
