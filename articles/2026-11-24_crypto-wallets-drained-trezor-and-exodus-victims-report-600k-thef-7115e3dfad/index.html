Crypto Wallets Drained: Trezor and Exodus Victims Report $600K Theft

Presence Editorial · November 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Onchain Lens, a blockchain analytics account, released an investigation on Nov. 23 detailing how a crypto wallet user lost over $600,000 from a Trezor Safe 7 and an Exodus wallet linked to the same iPhone device. The bulk of this loss, roughly $580,000 in USDT, resided on the TRON network, while analysts identified some stolen Solana USDC at an address connected to Bybit.

The investigation details indicate that the transfers examined utilized the victim's wallet authority, a method the user claims they never approved. The user stated to Onchain Lens that they had been operating the Trezor Safe 7 for about two months and connected it via Bluetooth to Trezor Suite on an iPhone 17 Pro Max, noting that the Exodus wallet was also installed on that specific device.

Hardware Wallets Drained Despite Sealed Packaging

An investigation is underway regarding a reported loss of over $600,000 from Trezor and Exodus wallets located on a single iPhone 17 Pro Max running iOS 27.2.1, with the attacker allegedly funded via @binance and some stolen funds directed to @Bybit_Official.

The user stated that the hardware wallet arrived sealed without visible tampering and displayed its recovery phrase on the Trezor screen during setup, noting they recorded the phrase on paper and never stored it digitally.

Trezor accounts on the TRON and Solana networks shared a single recovery phrase, meaning that a backup of that wallet could restore access to multiple accounts holding assets across different chains. This shared credential creates a direct link between the two Trezor losses that extends beyond the compromised phone itself, while the separate Exodus loss raises distinct questions regarding how attackers gained entry to that specific wallet.

Onchain Lens estimated the TRON-related loss at approximately $580,000 in USDT by comparing a transfer the user recognized with one reported as unauthorized. The disputed transaction occurred on November 21 and moved exactly 49,999 USDT directly from the wallet using the account's default owner permission. Following this initial movement, Onchain Lens noted that the TRON funds were subsequently distributed across several different addresses.

Understanding this difference is crucial since granting token approval enables a different address or contract to expend funds from a wallet based on current allowances. The TRON case reviewed by Onchain Lens demonstrated that the transfer depended on the account's inherent authority, with the network record confirming which permission was utilized. Conversely, the user's narrative of the incident stems from their independent declaration that they did not authorize the movement.

According to Onchain Lens, an additional $23,400 worth of USDC was extracted from the Trezor wallet on the Solana network. The investigation noted that the victim's address functioned as the transfer authority, signer, and fee payer for the transaction, with no separate delegate recorded in the instruction set. Furthermore, the firm disclosed a separate loss of $9,500 originating from the Exodus wallet. When these specific amounts are combined with other reported figures, the aggregate total reaches approximately $613,000, a sum where losses on the TRON blockchain represent nearly 95 percent.

Onchain Lens reported that the address it identified as the attacker's received approximately 0.76 SOL from Binance prior to the Solana sweep. The entity then traced the stolen USDC onward to an address it associated with Bybit. These transfers illustrate two distinct points in the fund trail: SOL arrived before the sweep, while some of the stolen USDC departed afterward.

Regarding the Solana USDC, Onchain Lens described a separate set of recipient addresses linked to the much larger TRON loss, while the victim stated they had contacted Bybit investigators about the matter. Exodus explained that its two wallets handle private keys differently, with the software wallet generating and encrypting private keys locally and storing them on the device.

Trezor states that its Safe 7 model retains private keys within protected hardware and utilizes a distinct chip to manage Bluetooth connectivity, allowing the companion phone to communicate with the device while ensuring transaction-signing keys remain on the Trezor unit itself.

Funds Traced to Exchanges via Unauthorized Transfers

Exodus documentation also references a Safe 7 integration that presents the hardware wallet as a portfolio view within its mobile application, though available reports did not confirm whether the user paired this specific device with Exodus. The incident involved a phone running iOS 27.2.1, a version Apple released on Nov. 9 to address security issues including Bluetooth vulnerabilities, yet Onchain Lens found no evidence linking those specific update issues to the wallet transfers.

Onchain Lens evaluated several potential explanations for the incident, including the exposure of recovery credentials and unauthorized signing. The victim stated that no other individual had physical access to their device and confirmed they had not approved the transfers. Investigators have yet to determine how the transactions obtained the wallets' signing authority or whether the two wallets were accessed in the same way.
