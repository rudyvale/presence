Stablecoin Reserves Plummet $16 Billion Amid Bitcoin Slump

Presence Editorial · November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Global stablecoin supply has contracted by approximately $16 billion over the past 30 days, a figure attributed to Nansen which also notes that Bitcoin recently reached a four-month low. During this same period, Ethereum is estimated to have comprised roughly three-quarters of the total decline in global stablecoin reserves measured by the firm.

Glassnode identified this as the quickest 30-day reduction in stablecoin holdings since June 2022 in its report published on Nov. 11. Concurrently, Bitget Wallet observed a 14.2% decrease in stablecoin balances between Sept. 30 and Nov. 16, while maintaining relatively steady levels in emerging markets. Despite the drop in transaction counts by 26%, swap volume on the Bitget platform increased by 24% during this timeframe.

Ethereum Drives Massive Supply Contraction

Nicolai Søndergaard, a senior research analyst at Nansen, stated that the total global stablecoin supply had dropped to $286 billion according to his firm's data as of Nov. 16. He pointed to declining cryptocurrency valuations and rising interest rates as the drivers behind this shift, noting that these factors reduced the incentive for traders to hold dollars on the blockchain while anticipating a better entry point. Søndergaard further clarified that the majority of funds exiting stablecoins are flowing back into cash instead of other tokens, since current market conditions render waiting on-chain less desirable.

Regarding the composition of the decline, Ethereum accounted for approximately $12 billion of the drop, representing roughly three-quarters of Nansen's global estimate. Søndergaard stated that large holders were redeeming Tether's USDT and Circle's USDC with their respective issuers, a process that removes tokens from circulation when holders exchange them for dollars. He clarified that a trade occurring between two holders leaves the tokens in circulation, distinguishing this activity from issuer redemptions which reduce the total supply.

According to a separate report released on Nov. 11, Glassnode recorded a $14.2 billion decrease in stablecoin supply over a 30-day period, marking the steepest drop in its historical series since June 2022. Bitget Wallet research analyst Lacie Zhang estimated the global decline at approximately $12 billion for the same timeframe, noting that her data covers holdings from the end of September through Nov. 16.

Søndergaard pointed out that the global supply shrinkage started before Binance suffered its security breach on Oct. 29. Nansen observed combined USDT and USDC balances on Ethereum drop from $39.5 billion prior to the incident down to $31.8 billion by Nov. 8, a decrease that includes stolen funds. Søndergaard stated these balances stayed level after Nov. 8.

Binance's November reserve snapshot indicated that customer USDT holdings decreased by 928.2 million tokens from Nov. 4 to Nov. 12, settling at 24.3 billion USDT after the eight-day period. During this same timeframe, Nansen tracked two tokens on Ethereum across a duration that includes the breach.

Zach Pandl of Grayscale noted that the reduction in USDC reserves began in early October and intensified after the Binance security breach, pointing to redemptions as a possible factor driving the broader cryptocurrency downturn along with declines in stocks tied to artificial intelligence and mandatory liquidations in futures markets.

Bitcoin dropped to $62,029 on Nov. 15, marking its lowest level since July 13. According to Bitfinex's report released on Nov. 16, outstanding futures positions decreased to 645,750 BTC during the weekend selloff, representing an 8% decline from levels recorded on Nov. 11. The exchange cited $2.01 billion in liquidations across crypto markets within a 24-hour period, noting that long positions accounted for 83% of the total volume.

Søndergaard observed that traders withdrew collateral from leveraged positions, causing stablecoin balances on Hyperliquid to fall by 17% over one month. He contrasted this with Bitcoin's decline of approximately 20% during the same timeframe and suggested that Treasury bills serve as an interest-bearing alternative for traders who might otherwise hold idle stablecoins.

Nansen recorded roughly $3 billion in redemptions during the latest seven-day period, a decrease from $4 billion the week prior, according to data where Søndergaard noted that supply growth returning would signal buyer recovery. Between Sept. 30 and Nov. 16, stablecoin holdings tracked by Bitget Wallet declined from $1.02 billion to $873.5 million, while average holdings per user dropped 16% over that interval.

Binance Breach and Market Volatility Impact Balances

Zhang stated that steadier balances in emerging markets may indicate demand for dollar savings and cross-border transfers rather than trading activity. She explained that users holding stablecoins for savings or international money transfers have less incentive to alter their balances when Bitcoin falls compared to traders maintaining stablecoins for upcoming trades.

According to Zhang, Bitget Wallet saw a 24% increase in total swap volume over the relevant timeframe despite a 26% decline in the number of transactions executed. This shift indicates that individual trades averaged 69% higher in value as larger sums moved through a reduced volume of activity.
