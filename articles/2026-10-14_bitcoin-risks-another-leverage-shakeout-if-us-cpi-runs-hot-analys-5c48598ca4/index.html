Bitcoin Leverage Remains in Focus Ahead of U.S. Inflation Data

Presence Editorial · October 14, 2026 · 3 min read

Analysts warned that a stronger U.S. inflation reading could expose Bitcoin to another selloff after a crypto liquidation wave totaling $811 million. Their assessment concerned the risk around the forthcoming Consumer Price Index release, rather than a decline that had already followed the data.

The liquidation episode left market-wide open interest at $140.9 billion, a reduction of only about 2%. The contrast mattered to the warning: a large liquidation total had accompanied a relatively small change in the amount of derivatives exposure still outstanding across the market.

Inflation expectations and price levels

Bitget Research’s Ryan Lee pointed to that remaining leverage as a vulnerability. His analysis linked the inflation release with the outlook for Treasury yields and Federal Reserve policy. Stronger inflation or renewed yield pressure were presented as possible challenges to market expectations, not certain outcomes.

Bitcoin was trading around $83,400–$83,500 in the pre-release assessment. Those prices provide the starting point for the analysts’ discussion of support and recovery levels. They describe the market at the time of the report and are separate from its conditional scenarios for subsequent moves.

The analysis identified $81,000–$82,000 as an initial support area, with the recent low near $80,400 serving as another downside reference. These were levels used to describe potential price pressure. Their inclusion did not establish that Bitcoin would fall to either area after the inflation release.

An upside band around $84,500–$85,000 also featured in the assessment of a possible recovery. The presence of both support and recovery references reflected the conditional nature of the outlook: the analysts were discussing alternative price responses, rather than reporting that either move had occurred.

Fund flows and derivatives exposure

Bitcoin ETF demand offered another part of the market picture. The assessment cited $175.7 million in inflows on October 13, following sizable outflows during the previous week. That change showed a return of fund inflows before the inflation release, alongside the remaining derivatives exposure.

ETF flows and open interest measure different things. Fund flows track money entering or leaving investment products, while open interest concerns outstanding derivatives contracts. A positive ETF-flow figure therefore does not show that leveraged positions have been unwound or that their liquidation risk has disappeared.

The distinction also applies to the $811 million liquidation figure and the 2% open-interest decline. Liquidations describe positions forced to close; open interest describes the contracts remaining outstanding. The reported liquidation amount was not a claim that a similar share of all market exposure had cleared.

Taken together, the figures described substantial remaining leverage, a rebound in ETF inflows and price levels on both sides of Bitcoin’s pre-release range. The central uncertainty was how inflation data and the market response would interact with those conditions; the report did not confirm another selloff.
