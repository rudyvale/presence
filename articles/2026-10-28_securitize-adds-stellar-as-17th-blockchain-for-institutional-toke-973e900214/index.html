Securitize Adds Stellar as 17th Blockchain for Institutional Tokenization

Presence Editorial · October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

On October 28, Securitize revealed intentions to bring the Stellar network into its tokenization framework, offering institutional asset managers a new blockchain choice for creating and overseeing regulated financial products. This move establishes Stellar as the platform's 17th supported blockchain dedicated to institutional tokenized assets, allowing the issuance and management of such regulated financial products on this new network.

Data from RedStone research shows that Stellar's market for tokenized real-world assets surpassed $3 billion in July. Meanwhile, Securitize noted roughly $5 billion in tokenized securities on its platform by September 30. Details regarding a specific asset, an institutional launch partner, or a completion date for the Stellar integration remain unannounced.

Strategic Expansion Amidst Growing Real-World Asset Market

Securitize announced today that Stellar will serve as its 17th supported blockchain for tokenized assets, a move revealed during Meridian 2026, the Stellar Development Foundation's two-day conference held in Lisbon, Portugal. The company provides tokenization and securities services to major institutions including BlackRock, Apollo, and Hamilton Lane within this framework.

The integration is designed to enable asset managers to issue, administer, and transfer tokenized financial instruments via Stellar while leveraging Securitize's existing securities infrastructure. Neither the companies have specified which financial products will launch through this partnership nor have they provided a completion date for the implementation.

At the Meridian2026 event, Securitize revealed a partnership with Stellar to broaden the range of blockchain tools accessible to asset managers who wish to move traditional financial offerings onto a distributed ledger. This collaboration marks Stellar as the seventeenth platform within the firm's suite for tokenized assets. The arrangement allows these managers to create, manage, and move tokenized securities using Stellar while relying on Securitize's current securities framework. Details regarding specific products slated for launch or a timeline for rollout remain undisclosed by either party.

Carlos Domingo, co-founder and chief executive of Securitize, noted that Stellar has cultivated what the firm views as the fourth-largest environment for tokenized real-world assets. He expressed enthusiasm about connecting with the Stellar network to extend Securitize's compliant infrastructure toward an ecosystem actively promoting the uptake of tokenized financial instruments. The company maintains that its regulated subsidiaries continue to offer brokerage, transfer agency, and investment advisory capabilities in the United States, as well as licensed securities functions in Europe. Furthermore, this technical connection does not alter the investor qualification, regulatory adherence, or custody obligations inherent to specific financial products.

Securitize declared a collaboration with Stellar to function as its seventeenth platform for holding tokenized assets, an initiative crafted to bolster a multichain approach that permits asset managers to choose networks aligned with their distinct operational and distribution needs. The firm noted that the announcement did not include any figures regarding transaction volume, deployment expenses, or anticipated revenue impact. Prior to this development, Stellar was already accommodating investment vehicles from prominent financial entities, such as Franklin Templeton's tokenized U.S. Government Money Fund, whereas the BENJI fund commenced operations on the network in 2021 and later broadened its presence to other blockchains.

Research conducted in August regarding Stellar's market for tokenized assets indicated that the value of real-world assets rose from roughly $785 million in January to exceeding $3 billion by July. These statistics originated from RedStone research which examined tokenized funds, Treasuries, and corporate credit.

According to the same research, significant holdings were found in products from Spiko, Ondo Finance, and Franklin Templeton; however, the value of assets issued on a network is distinct from the value actively traded or utilized as collateral. RedStone's analysis revealed that lending pools on Blend accepting real-world assets held slightly more than $2 million, even though Stellar's base of tokenized assets runs into the multibillions. These figures indicate that growth in issuance and adoption within decentralized lending are separate metrics.

Stellar offers asset controls including identity verification, transfer restrictions, and clawbacks for regulated products. Denelle Dixon, who serves as CEO and executive director of the Stellar Development Foundation, welcomed the partnership as a chance to develop further institutional products. "We are excited to collaborate to broaden the infrastructure accessible to asset managers," Dixon said.

Following a series of institutional initiatives on other blockchain networks, Securitize has signed a new agreement; in September, KB Securities chose Securitize and Optimism to create tokenized funds for South Korean institutions, starting with a proposed money market product. In contrast to the Stellar announcement, the Korean deal specifies OP Mainnet as the network for its initial fund, and neither announcement confirms that the proposed products have started trading.

Separately, Securitize has confirmed that 12 tokenized stocks are currently live on Solana for eligible investors; the October offering features representations of shares in companies including Apple, Nvidia, and Tesla, with participation contingent upon onboarding and applicable jurisdictional restrictions.

Clarifications on Infrastructure and Compliance

During the month of August, Securitize launched a tokenized high-yield fund in partnership with Neuberger, aiming to distribute it on Avalanche, Ethereum, Solana, and Sui networks. This specific offering focuses on corporate credit investments and limits participation to qualified investors only. By September 30, the firm disclosed roughly $5 billion in tokenized securities hosted on its platform; this total reflects assets within its broader operations rather than tokens specifically issued via Stellar.

No asset manager has been identified as committed to the upcoming Stellar integration, nor have initial supported securities been specified. The declaration released on October 28 keeps both the timeline for integration and specifics of individual products undefined. Furthermore, forward-looking statements included with this announcement caution that execution might face delays, modifications, or remain unfinished due to technical hurdles, regulatory requirements, or commercial factors.
