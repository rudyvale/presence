Israel Discount Bank Launches Crypto Trading Ahead of Rivals

Presence Editorial · November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Israel Discount Bank announced on Nov. 11 that eligible adult customers will be able to buy, hold, and sell Bitcoin and Ether through its existing mobile app starting Nov. 26, with tax handling integrated into their investment accounts. Two cryptocurrencies are set to be available via the platform, while six months of account management fees will be waived for this service, though specific trading rates remain undisclosed.

To handle assets stored in its own wallets, the bank intends to employ Fireblocks infrastructure so that funds from sales go straight into customer accounts. This rollout is designed to happen before Bank Leumi introduces its crypto trading service, a move Globes said would occur in early 2027. The new feature enables clients to buy and sell cryptocurrencies within the same banking interface used for stocks and bonds, removing the requirement to set up an external exchange account or maintain a separate digital wallet.

Bank Integrates Crypto into Existing App

Eligibility for the new crypto trading capability is confined to private individuals aged 18 and above who maintain accounts authorized for securities transactions, in accordance with the bank's specific terms and enrollment agreement. Digital holdings will reside within bank-managed wallets utilizing Fireblocks infrastructure, ensuring that proceeds from asset sales transfer directly to the client's bank account.

Elad Fisher, who heads Discount's Financial Markets Division, spearheaded this project, while Group CEO Avi Levi characterized the launch as a foundational move to build out the institution's digital currency capabilities and infrastructure. Levi noted that clients can execute cryptocurrency trades from their existing accounts, eliminating the requirement for complicated, separate digital wallets.

Israel Discount Bank has introduced Bitcoin and Ether trading within its mobile application, a development spearheaded by Elad Fisher of the Financial Markets Division under Group CEO Avi Levi. The institution described this launch as a foundational step to expand its digital currency infrastructure, allowing clients to execute trades directly from existing accounts without requiring complex, separate digital wallets. In promoting the service, the bank stated that trading commissions are low but did not disclose specific rates in its announcement, while simultaneously waiving account management fees for six months. However, the bank issued a warning regarding the exceptional risks associated with cryptocurrency trading, explicitly citing high volatility and the potential for losing the entire investment.

Regarding regulatory and operational frameworks, the tax authority permits investors to offset crypto capital gains and losses against other portfolio holdings before deducting taxes at the source. Globes underscored this approach by contrasting it with external platforms where individuals must manage their own tax responsibilities. The outlet further observed that Israeli investors have encountered obstacles when attempting to deposit proceeds from cryptocurrency activities into commercial bank accounts, as banks demand proof of fund origins to comply with anti-money laundering regulations.

The Israel Tax Authority has established a specific procedure for individuals to pay taxes on cryptocurrency profits after commercial banks refuse to process the relevant funds or open accounts. This official route requires that the money used for tax payment originates from lawful sources tied to the original asset purchase, with no alternative funding permitted for this purpose. The service is strictly unavailable to companies, limiting eligibility solely to individual investors facing these banking restrictions.

To utilize this mechanism, applicants must submit their taxable-income calculation, proof of the original funds used for the digital assets, a history of those assets during ownership, and confirmation that the bank refused the transaction. This procedure was developed in collaboration with the Bank of Israel and the Money Laundering Authority to enable tax funds to be transferred into the authority's account at the central bank.

On August 14, Bank Leumi revealed a collaboration with Galaxy to facilitate transactions in Bitcoin, Ether, and Solana via its Leumi Trade platform. The initiative is slated for deployment by early 2027, serving both Leumi and PEPPER account holders. GalaxyOne Institutional will handle the trading operations, whereas custody functions will be managed by Galaxy's Custody Infrastructure platform, which was previously known as GK8.

According to Globes, Discount Bank is positioned to initiate its rollout ahead of competitors provided it adheres to its timeline. The outlet highlighted that a prior partnership between Bank Leumi and Paxos, dubbed Pepper Invest, was unveiled in March 2022 but ultimately failed to achieve a comprehensive market launch.

Eligibility, Infrastructure and Fee Waiver Details

Bits of Gold operates under financial services license 56716 issued by Israel's Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority, with its regulatory page detailing a separate agreement with the Tax Authority for crypto tax calculations and payments. The company secured approval for its BILS shekel stablecoin following a two-year Solana pilot, and reserve arrangements mandate designated, separate accounts in Israel.

Beyond Israel's borders, Deutsche Bank introduced crypto custody services on November 11 for institutional and corporate clients across Europe. The initial portfolio includes Bitcoin, Ether, USDC, EURC, and EURAU, with the institution retaining control of private keys. This offering does not yet include retail accounts, though the bank intends to extend operations to Asian financial hubs by 2027.
