Monero FCMP++ Upgrade Passes zkSecurity Audit with No Critical Flaws

Presence Editorial · November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Monero (XMR) has finished reviewing the security of its Generalized Bulletproofs code ahead of the Full-Chain Membership Proofs (FCMP++) release, and investigators detected no flaws rated as high or medium risk. The pending FCMP++ update successfully passed a zkSecurity review without any critical or significant weaknesses being found.

Investigators spotted four minor problems alongside three informational notes, each addressed by the monero-oxide development team. A machine-checked mathematical verification from zkSecurity validated that the Generalized Bulletproofs protocol maintains completeness, zero-knowledge properties, and soundness. This FCMP++ enhancement aims to swap out Monero's current ring signature architecture for membership proofs that encompass every output on the blockchain.

Audit Results and Developer Responses

A notice issued by MAGIC Grants on November 12 details that zkSecurity dedicated ten business days to examining the cryptographic protocol and its code, uncovering four low-severity problems alongside three informational notes. Following this review, teams working on the monero-oxide initiative addressed every one of the seven identified points, finishing their efforts on the generalized-bulletproofs module slated for inclusion in Monero's upcoming significant privacy enhancement.

On November 18, Monero issued a declaration through X confirming the audit's successful conclusion and drawing attention to the supplementary mathematical validation performed by zkSecurity, which verified three key security attributes of the system. The organization noted that this validation demonstrates the Generalized Bulletproofs protocol, as deployed within Monero, possesses completeness, zero-knowledge characteristics, and soundness, referencing data released by MAGIC Grants.

In June, MAGIC Grants engaged zkSecurity to evaluate the generalized-bulletproofs crate inside monero-oxide, which serves as a Rust-based realization of Monero's cryptographic elements. Mathias Hall-Andersen and Suneal Gong led this evaluation, scrutinizing both the foundational cryptography and its specific implementation.

Mathias Hall-Andersen and Suneal Gong, who examined the cryptography and implementation alongside MAGIC Grants' June commission of zkSecurity to review monero-oxide's generalized-bulletproofs crate, crafted a novel mathematical proof. This proof establishes knowledge soundness, guaranteeing that a valid proof cannot be generated without the necessary underlying information. Furthermore, Generalized Bulletproofs adapt the original Bulletproofs system to accommodate Pedersen vector commitments within more intricate cryptographic structures.

The concept first appeared in a Curve Trees document written by Matteo Campanelli, Hall-Andersen, and Simon Holmgaard Kamp. Aaron Feickert, who was working with Cypher Stack at the time, built upon that initial idea to create a full technical outline supported by security proofs. Later, Brandon Goodell, also from Cypher Stack, found errors within those proofs and suggested fixes.

After the code review concluded, zkSecurity utilized Lean, a theorem prover enabling computers to verify individual steps in mathematical arguments, to independently formalize the protocol's mathematical properties. The researchers made their work available via a public GitHub repository, addressing completeness, zero-knowledge, and soundness. Initially discussed in a Curve Trees paper by Matteo Campanelli, Hall-Andersen, and Simon Holmgaard Kamp, the approach was later expanded by Cypher Stack cryptographer Aaron Feickert into a full technical description with security proofs. Subsequently, Brandon Goodell, another Cypher Stack researcher, spotted issues in those proofs and suggested corrections.

The formal verification process defined the interactive protocol within an abstract mathematical group, while the written audit specifically addressed the implementation and related cryptographic components. Completeness ensures that valid proofs are accepted, zero-knowledge guarantees that the verification process does not disclose secret information being proved, and soundness prevents invalid statements from being accepted as legitimate.

While the formalization process included an analysis of two more efficient variants of Generalized Bulletproofs, Monero currently has no intention of implementing either option. This review represents a component of Monero's ongoing efforts regarding FCMP++, a scheduled network upgrade designed to alter the method by which the blockchain verifies that spent funds originate from its established transaction history.

Monero's second FCMP++ beta stressnet launched on May 6 at block height 2,997,100, marking a shift from ring signatures that conceal specific transaction outputs to membership proofs covering the blockchain's entire output set. This architectural change aims to replace the smaller anonymity groups currently utilized by ring signatures with a broader verification method.

Mathematical Verification of Security Properties

Trail of Bits performed an independent security review of the FCMP++ cryptographic code on Aug. 17, a process that also included a distinct evaluation by MAGIC Grants. Under this new framework, transactions can access an anonymity pool comprising roughly 150 million current outputs, thereby broadening the reach beyond the smaller groups used by ring signatures.

Community financial support has underpinned the development process concurrent with these evaluations. During October, a Monero contributor sought 45 XMR to sustain three months of labor focused on core software enhancements, wallet refinements, and privacy infrastructure. Earlier, Monero's Community Crowdfunding System allocated 500 XMR for a distinct audit of FCMP++ integration, directing contributions toward the examination of suggested network modifications. The initiative persists in depending on community-sustained development proposals to advance protocol research, execution tasks, and standalone security evaluations.
