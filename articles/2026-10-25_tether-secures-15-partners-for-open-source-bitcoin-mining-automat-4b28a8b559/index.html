Tether Secures 15 Partners for Open-Source Bitcoin Mining Automation

Presence Editorial · October 25, 2026 · 5 min read

As the stablecoin issuer broadens its efforts in AI-driven mining automation, Tether has obtained pledges from fifteen entities comprising Bitcoin mining firms, hardware producers, and mining pools to back its open-source Mining Development Kit (MDK). The organization notes that these fifteen industry groups have formally endorsed the project.

Among the participants are Bitmain, Bitdeer, Canaan, MicroBT, Foundry Digital, and F2Pool, with each expected to develop an integration connecting its mining systems to the shared software. Tether's QVAC mining agent is already operating on the MDK, while the stable 1.0 release of the kit is targeted for the end of 2026.

Industry Giants Join Tether's AI Mining Initiative

According to Tether's announcement on October 24, fifteen Bitcoin mining firms have joined its MDK platform to create and sustain software interfaces that enable diverse mining hardware and services to function within a unified system. This collaborative initiative encompasses major equipment manufacturers and mining pools, with the first stable version of the software scheduled for release by the end of 2026.

The roster of participants includes Bitmain, Bitdeer, Canaan, MicroBT, Foundry Digital, F2Pool, Luxor Technology, OCEAN, ACME Swisstech, Elektron Energy, EMCD, Heatbit, Proto, SpiderPool, and Stratum V2. These entities span the full spectrum of the mining sector, involving companies that manufacture equipment, operate facilities, and manage pools for distributing miner rewards, thereby integrating various industry segments into a single development effort.

In April, Tether launched MDK, a framework designed for open-source Bitcoin mining that enables developers to oversee hardware and construct applications without depending solely on vendor-supplied software. While recent news highlights pledges to create and sustain integrations, it remains unclear whether every one of the 15 firms has finished linking up or rolled out MDK within their own mining setups.

Specific data regarding deployment levels, performance metrics, or financial efficiencies were not shared for these entities. Major Bitcoin mining sites frequently house thousands of units from various makers, meaning that machinery, cooling infrastructure, and mining pools often rely on distinct management tools, forcing staff to toggle between different systems or engineer bespoke links.

Mining operations typically house vast arrays of hardware sourced from various producers. Because cooling units, mining pools, and machinery often rely on distinct management programs, staff must navigate multiple platforms or engineer bespoke links. MDK tackles this challenge via modular software elements known as Workers. Every partner firm will craft a Worker capable of converting data from its specific gear or services into a language readable by MDK-based applications. Consequently, an operator can deploy unified monitoring and automation utilities across heterogeneous hardware without reconstructing the whole application for each maker.

This partnership leaves firms free to keep their current machinery and retain authority over their operating systems, since members decide which capabilities to expose via the common gateway. Paolo Ardoino, chief executive of Tether, observed that software is shifting toward directly managing physical assets rather than just reporting on them.

Paolo Ardoino, chief executive of Tether, stated that software is increasingly shifting toward directly managing physical assets rather than merely reporting on them. This framework follows Tether's decision in February to release its Mining OS as open-source software, a system designed to manage mining hardware, energy use, and operational information while the MDK provides tools for developers to build additional applications. Ardoino explained that the initiative aims to ensure every machine speaks a common language at scale, requiring mining operators and equipment manufacturers to adopt shared software interfaces.

Tether anticipates that this shared interface will enable AI tools to oversee hardware, detect malfunctions, and modify workflows in response to shifting circumstances. The firm points out that its QVAC mining agent is currently running directly on MDK, illustrating how AI software can interact with mining infrastructure without needing a distinct link for each unit. This setup enables participating companies to maintain control of their operating systems and existing machinery, allowing members to determine which features are exposed through the common gateway.

The announcement regarding Tether's collaboration with fifteen Bitcoin mining firms for AI automation tools did not include independent verification of specific performance metrics such as reductions in electricity consumption, increases in mining output, or decreases in maintenance costs. Ardoino reinforced the company's statement on X, characterizing the initiative as a unified development platform intended to manage all mining operations, though no external data confirmed the agent's actual impact on these operational parameters.

This mining initiative is situated within Tether's broader efforts to develop AI infrastructure, following its participation in Tenstorrent's funding round which secured $1.5 billion at a valuation of $6.2 billion. While Tether did not disclose the specific amount of its individual investment in the chipmaker, its AI development portfolio includes software capable of running models on personal devices without reliance on centralized cloud services, distinguishing its approach from traditional cloud-dependent computing architectures.

MDK Framework Targets Unified Equipment Management

During the Plan ₿ Forum in Lugano, which took place from October 23 to 24, MDK was expected to hit version 0.11. Tether indicated that its initial stable release, designated as 1.0, is still aimed at coming out prior to the close of 2026. The framework is currently available for developers wishing to test integrations and construct applications. Furthermore, the organization has invited further involvement from hardware makers, mining pools, site managers, and software creators.

A toolkit built with JavaScript and a React interface library for custom dashboards and device management arrived in April. Tether noted that this software functions across Windows, macOS, and Linux systems. Deployment options span from residential mining gear up to extensive industrial installations.
