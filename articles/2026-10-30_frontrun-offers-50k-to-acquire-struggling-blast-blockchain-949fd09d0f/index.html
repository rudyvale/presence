Frontrun Offers $50k to Acquire Struggling Blast Blockchain

Presence Editorial · October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Crypto trading platform Frontrun.pro has publicly approached the team behind Blast with a proposal to acquire the company, initiating discussions with an opening offer of $50,000. This figure is explicitly described as a starting point for negotiations, with no final purchase price currently disclosed. In making this public request, Frontrun claims to possess a prepared team and prospective investors ready to support the blockchain infrastructure.

Frontrun.pro detailed its interest in an X post dated Oct. 29, stating it had individuals ready to work on the network while seeking confirmation from Blast's leadership regarding a potential sale. The proposal comes after Blast previously announced a wind-down plan, citing that operating costs exceeded revenue generated by its Layer 2 solution. Prospective investors mentioned in the offer remain unnamed, and their participation is conditional upon the successful completion of a company sale.

Public Acquisition Proposal Lacks Financial Details

On October 29, Frontrun.pro directed an inquiry to Blast's official account proposing the acquisition of the company for $50,000. The message stated that a team was prepared to improve the blockchain and that several investors were ready to provide capital, but emphasized that these funds would only be deployed after the company changed hands.

The offer failed to disclose the legal entity behind it, identify potential investors, or outline a schedule for the deal. It framed the investment as conditional on a prior sale, requiring Blast to transfer ownership before funds could be injected. This message was sent directly to Blast's account and provided a specific contact address for acquisitions, yet it omitted details about who was making the offer or when the transaction might occur.

Frontrun stated in a message that it was prepared to offer $50,000 to initiate the deal, though the wording did not clarify whether this sum represented an initial payment, a negotiating figure, or the full consideration sought. The communication provided no details regarding ownership percentages, payment structures, or specific assets intended for acquisition.

While Frontrun asserted that it could improve the blockchain ten times over, it failed to supply a technical plan, performance targets, or a budget to support that claim. The post offered no description of proposed changes to validators, infrastructure, applications, or the BLAST token.

No signed agreement or acceptance from Blast accompanied the announcement; instead, it solicited discussions and listed an email for correspondence. A previous report indicated that earlier in the month, Blast declared its closure because network maintenance expenses surpassed the revenue generated by the Layer 2. The message contained no signed pact nor confirmation from Blast, merely requesting talks via a provided email address. Earlier this month, reports stated that Blast had announced its shutdown after its team noted that sustaining the network cost more than the income produced by the Layer 2.

Blast stated in its Oct. 2 notice that no viable path to economic sustainability existed and urged users to move assets to the Ethereum mainnet, including those held in the progressive web app and on the network.

Blast's operational timeline included a specific cutoff where Oct. 26 was established as the deadline for utilizing the standard withdrawal interface, with instructions to be provided prior to that date. Following this period, assets remained accessible via direct interaction with bridge contracts on Ethereum. The team subsequently planned to withdraw Blast's Lido assets before resuming withdrawals under a shorter 24-hour delay, estimating this process would require approximately one week during which user withdrawals would be temporarily unavailable.

Separately, Blast's public status page indicated that its mainnet, block production, public RPC, and batch submission were operational when checked on Oct. 29, with no incidents listed for that date. Founded by Blur co-founder Tieshun Roquerre, known as Pacman, the platform initially accepted deposits before its mainnet was available.

Blast Faces Shutdown After Revenue Shortfall

Data from DeFiLlama shows Blast's deposits exceeded $720 million by December 2023, a time when participants funded accounts via multisignature contracts to earn yields on their crypto holdings. The initiative merged a native-yield approach with expectations of a future token distribution, yet its initial setup faced examination due to the lack of an active mainnet and dependence on a multisignature wallet architecture.

By May 2025, Blast discontinued its partnership with Safe, pointing to risks associated with third parties and usability issues as it developed internal multisignature capabilities. Individuals utilizing Safe's interface for access were rerouted to BrahmaFi's platform or permitted to establish their own setups, while Blast noted it was embedding multisignature features directly within the Blast Mobile application.
