Deribit Restarts Trading Following Scheduled Patch and Market Disruption

Presence Editorial · November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Deribit, a platform that operates alongside Coinbase-owned exchanges, restarted trading operations on November 20 following an outage that impacted crypto perpetuals, stock-linked contracts, and cryptocurrency transfers. An incident report identified Bitcoin and Ether perpetuals, crypto transfer services, and several trading APIs as components affected by the disruption. The list of affected items also included contracts tied to Nvidia, Tesla, gold, and silver alongside various crypto markets.

Deribit had installed a scheduled security patch earlier in the week before announcing the conclusion of the trading pause at 09:06 UTC. According to Deribit's status page, trading was unavailable at 16:17 UTC while the exchange investigated the issue; by 16:36 UTC, the platform declared that trading had resumed and shifted the incident to a monitoring phase, with the two notices issued just 19 minutes apart.

Scheduled Maintenance Triggers Platform-Wide Pause

An incident report detailed the impact on Deribit's proprietary trading interface, its FIX order and market data APIs, REST API, WebSocket feed, and the FIX drop-copy service that delivers separate streams of execution records to firms. The exchange's earliest statement confirmed that trading was inaccessible across the entire platform.

Trading interruptions impacted perpetual futures for Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, and XRP, as well as BTC-USDC and ETH-USDC pairs. Deribit also cited equity-linked contracts based on Nvidia, Tesla, Apple, and Amazon, alongside perpetuals linked to S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 ETFs like SPY and QQQ. The report further noted disruptions involving gold and silver perpetuals.

Deribit confirmed that trading resumed at 16:36 UTC following an outage that had previously affected Bitcoin, Ether, and XRP on their respective networks, along with USDC transfers across seven networks including Ethereum, Solana, Base, and Arbitrum. The update also listed EURC transfers separately for Base and Ethereum, while Optimism, Polygon, and Avalanche C-Chain completed the list of supported chains for USDC. At the time of the 16:36 UTC announcement, the exchange stated it was keeping the incident under monitoring but did not provide specific recovery times for individual transfer routes.

In its Telegram announcements, Deribit explained that a deployment scheduled for 09:00 UTC was intended to apply a security patch addressing a recently disclosed vulnerability. The exchange anticipated that this maintenance would pause trading for less than five minutes and assured users that open orders would be preserved throughout the scheduled work period.

Deribit informed API users that its gateways would restart sequentially, warning that connections might drop and responses could briefly slow during the process. The exchange requested that software be configured to reconnect automatically and posted that trading resumed at 09:06 UTC, with the status page marking maintenance completion at 09:15 UTC.

An independent monitoring service known as up24 detected disruptions within Deribit's Bitcoin order book feed during the morning trading session. The provider observed that its WebSocket verifications regarding the Bitcoin perpetual order book halted at 09:00 UTC before functionality returned at 09:10 UTC. While up24 monitored a single market-data stream, Deribit's official reopening statement addressed trading broadly; subsequent updates in the afternoon failed to specify the root cause of the event.

Coinbase stated that its new options product, which utilizes Deribit's infrastructure and settles contracts in USDC, became available to eligible customers in select non-U.S. markets on a day following the announcement reported by the report on Nov. 19; the launch included Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, Avalanche, Hyperliquid, and XRP.

Coinbase Advanced enables eligible customers to combine multiple options contracts within a single order, such as purchasing one Bitcoin call while simultaneously selling another at a higher strike price, with the platform displaying estimated margin and fees before submission and pricing the combination in USDC.

Coinbase's integration guide indicates that on Oct. 1, positions and balances for its international exchange customers were transferred to Deribit, while existing clients on the platform received access to over 100 additional perpetual contracts covering equity and commodity products. Following this initial expansion, Coinbase subsequently mapped a pathway for US institutions to utilize Coinbase Prime and its futures brokerage arm, Coinbase Financial Markets, directing eligible entities to contact their account managers regarding specific onboarding procedures and timing.

Prior to the commencement of morning maintenance, Deribit executed an eight-for-one adjustment on its perpetual contract linked to SanDisk stock. This development followed Coinbase's international business launching stock-linked perpetual futures for eligible non-US customers in March, a product suite that included contracts on seven individual stocks and two ETFs at the time. The company later filed in September to extend this specific type of contract offering to the US market.

Broad Impact Across Crypto and Equity Markets

A distinct announcement released for Friday detailed Deribit's plan to modify the minimum price tick sizes available for USDC-denominated Bitcoin options and various other instruments, such as silver perpetual futures. The platform clarified that these modifications would impact both standalone order books and mixed contract portfolios.

According to a Telegram message posted at 01:59 UTC, Deribit confirmed the reopening of trading for the SanDisk instrument. To align with the recent stock split, the exchange increased position quantities by a factor of eight while reducing entry and mark prices by the same amount.
