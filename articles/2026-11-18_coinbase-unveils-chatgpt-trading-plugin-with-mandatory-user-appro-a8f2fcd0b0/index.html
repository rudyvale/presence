Coinbase Unveils ChatGPT Trading Plugin with Mandatory User Approval

Presence Editorial · November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Coinbase has launched a ChatGPT plugin that enables eligible US customers to trade stocks, crypto, and derivatives within a conversational interface, with the requirement that users must approve every order themselves. This integration supports three distinct asset categories, though availability is subject to the customer's specific location and their Coinbase account eligibility status. The system utilizes confirmation cards to cover new orders, edits, cancellations, and conversions between dollars and USDC, while allowing users to choose which portfolios to connect; Coinbase assigns responsibility for agent trades directly to the customers.

Coinbase introduced a new plugin for ChatGPT on November 17, distinct from its proprietary AI integration designed for automated trading sequences. The company informed clients that they would be able to link their accounts, oversee portfolios, and authorize transactions within the chat interface used for general inquiries. Brian Armstrong, the firm's chief executive, urged customers to experiment with constructing a portfolio using artificial intelligence and executing trades directly through their Coinbase profiles.

Eligibility and Asset Scope

The exchange highlighted support for stocks, cryptocurrencies, and derivatives, noting the setup process requires merely minutes. Starting today anyone with ChatGPT can connect their Coinbase account to leverage artificial intelligence for constructing personalized portfolios and executing trades directly within the platform.

As of today, individuals utilizing ChatGPT may link their Coinbase accounts to leverage artificial intelligence for constructing personalized portfolios and executing trades directly within the platform, a setup process noted as requiring merely minutes. The exchange's initial announcement highlighted that coverage ranges from financial discussions to actual capital deployment, encompassing stocks, cryptocurrencies, and derivatives. Coinbase documentation indicates that the official plugin is accessible to customers in the United States, contingent upon their specific ChatGPT account and workspace. Users must log into Coinbase to examine requested permissions and select which portfolios the connection may access.

Interactive cards within ChatGPT on web or mobile apps display order details for confirmation, a requirement that extends to changes, cancellations, new trades, and dollar–USDC conversions. Requests limited to retrieving information do not require this approval card.

Coinbase separates this new integration from its separate Model Context Protocol link, which enables automated processes without needing permission for every single transaction. While the custom connection provides access to global derivatives and paid research via x402, the specific ChatGPT trading setup does not include those x402 capabilities.

Coinbase states that linking an account to its ChatGPT trading plugin does not grant access to every product within the ecosystem, noting that asset availability is contingent upon regional restrictions and specific account eligibility criteria such as those for futures markets. The exchange's product page indicates that establishing most agent connections requires no coding or API keys, allowing customers to simply add Coinbase to their preferred AI application, sign in, and then explicitly decide which assets the agent can access.

On matters of security, the exchange states that linked agents are barred from transferring money outside the Coinbase ecosystem and that users keep the power to sever their connection whenever they choose. The company advises allocating a distinct portfolio to an agent, holding exclusively the funds intended for it; this safeguard was already integrated into its AI trading offering in September, allowing clients to limit permissions to a funded account while supported stocks and derivatives continued to face specific eligibility rules.

Coinbase states that its agent service imposes no additional fee beyond standard trading charges for executed trades, noting that operational capabilities depend on the specific application where chat tools function during conversations while others support scheduled activity. The launch's legal notice characterizes the service as informational only, explicitly stating it does not provide investment advice or recommendations, with product availability and eligibility varying by jurisdiction.

The accompanying legal documentation warns that AI agents may fail and places full responsibility for an agent's trades and third-party data usage on the user who directs the activity. Coinbase's public software repository further cautions that agents can generate inaccurate or unexpected output and may be influenced by third-party material they read, treating all instructions sent through these tools as originating from the customer.

Coinbase introduced a ChatGPT trading plugin that requires explicit user approval for every order, building on the June 11 launch of Coinbase for Agents which enabled external assistants to connect with customer accounts for trading and portfolio management. The new tool operates within a framework where the company's public software repository warns that AI agents may fail and can produce inaccurate or unexpected output while being influenced by third-party material they read.

Safety Controls and Liability

Legal documents accompanying the release state that customers directing AI actions bear complete liability for an agent's trades and how third-party data is utilized, effectively treating every instruction passed through these tools as coming from the user. This method builds on the Nov. 11 introduction of Agent Controls, a framework that set protections such as a per-trade dollar limit, a rolling 24-hour trading-volume ceiling, and an automatic stop linked to portfolio declines.

Coinbase's ChatGPT trading plugin operates with controls hosted on the company's servers that agents cannot modify independently, requiring customers to manage settings via an account secured by a password and two-factor authentication. The system halts trade execution automatically when a portfolio reaches its selected loss threshold, resuming only after the customer re-enables trading, while Coinbase has indicated that options for selecting between crypto, equities, and derivatives are scheduled for future release.
