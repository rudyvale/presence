Binance Dismisses ShinyHunters Data Breach Claims as Likely Scam

Presence Editorial · November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Binance has rejected allegations that hackers successfully extracted 335000000 user records from the cryptocurrency exchange, stating that its preliminary investigation indicates the data listing is likely a scam. The firm noted that ShinyHunters' assertion regarding the theft of 335 million user records appears fraudulent because the initial probe found no evidence of compromised systems or customer data.

Regarding the specific demands made by the threat actor, they allegedly requested $900,000 in Bitcoin or Monero for a dataset purportedly containing user credentials, identity documents, transaction histories and private wallet keys. A Binance spokesperson further questioned the credibility of this listing, pointing to inconsistencies such as the presence of plaintext passwords, external wallet private keys, and references to jurisdictions where the exchange no longer operates.

Binance Rejects Alleged Data Theft as Scam

According to a Binance representative, the platform is currently examining claims posted on a hacking forum and X about a purported user dataset. An early assessment of the shared details failed to uncover proof of a fresh intrusion affecting its infrastructure or client information. The official posited that such data might mimic records harvested by infostealer malware aimed at personal gadgets instead of originating from Binance's internal networks.

The representative added that, given the provided descriptions and the limited samples examined up to this point, the scenario seems more indicative of fraud than a genuine breach of Binance systems.

A company representative stated that their examination revealed no signs that personal identification papers, login details, secret wallet keys, or internal system data were compromised via the promoted file set. The spokesperson noted that multiple specifics within the offered list are inconsistent with Binance's methods for handling client data and running its crypto operations.

A key issue centers on assertions that the purportedly stolen files include user account passcodes. Binance clarified it keeps no unencrypted versions of these passwords, a fact that clashes with how the alleged data was characterized. The listing also alleges possession of private keys for external cryptocurrency wallets; however, the firm explained it does not retain such keys for outside wallets, casting doubt on that specific accusation.

Binance stated that the geographical scope of the alleged breach includes jurisdictions where the exchange no longer operates, creating a significant discrepancy with its current operational footprint. The firm's spokesperson suggested that the advertised records likely resemble data collected by infostealer malware targeting individual devices rather than a direct intrusion into Binance systems.

After ThreatMon, a cybersecurity monitoring entity, disclosed information about an underground forum post attributed to the threat actor ShinyHunters, details emerged concerning a listing that allegedly held 335,900,000 lines of data connected to Binance users from approximately 170 nations. A company representative noted that comparable previous listings frequently feature infostealer malware data gathered from users' own devices instead of indicating a breach of Binance systems.

The dataset in question is said to include a wide array of sensitive data, such as email addresses, phone numbers, passwords, identification documents, transaction records, and various other personal details. ShinyHunters also asserted that the compromised information comprised internal infrastructure logs, private wallet keys, and specifics regarding cryptocurrency assets stored in Binance's hot wallets. The group requested a ransom of $900,000, payable in Bitcoin or Monero, for the data and issued a warning that they would publish the dataset if their demands were ignored.

Within the forum advertisement, the actor reportedly cautioned Binance that it faced only two remaining choices: pay the ransom or suffer a data leak. ThreatMon noted that the seller had made a sample of the alleged records available behind a paywall on the forum but emphasized that the breach has not been independently verified, especially concerning the claims about Binance's hot wallets and private keys.

A security monitoring entity flagged ShinyHunters as a collective that had earlier promoted claims of data theft affecting Salesforce Data.com, the Indian retailer Vijay Sales, and Kuwaiti government bodies. These accusations emerged just days following a distinct, verified incident at Binance on October 29, during which intruders removed roughly $6.34 billion in digital assets from its hot wallets.

According to the November 3 disclosure, Binance determined that the intrusion stemmed from infected staff terminals, harmful code, and pilfered login details that granted illicit entry into wallet signing systems. Following this discovery, the platform noted that about $2.85 billion was halted or caught, and it deployed internal funds to restore impacted user holdings.

Technical Discrepancies Undermine Breach Claims

Following an incident on November 5, Binance sought to recover 8,425.997 ETH and roughly 74.56 million ARB that were immobilized after the assault. This recent ShinyHunters announcement, which claims customer data and login details were stolen, marks a separate event from a prior case where cryptocurrency moved without authorization.

Addressing phishing attempts that mimic official exchange notifications and steer users toward fake sites meant to harvest credentials, a company representative advised the public to exercise vigilance. They specifically warned against sharing passwords, 2FA codes, wallet seed phrases, or private keys in response to such suspicious outreach.
