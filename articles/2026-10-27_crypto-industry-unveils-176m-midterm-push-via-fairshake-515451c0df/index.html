Crypto Industry Unveils $176M Midterm Push via Fairshake

Presence Editorial · October 27, 2026 · 5 min read

The U.S. crypto industry has allocated approximately $176 million across more than 100 congressional races in preparation for the November 3 midterm elections, aiming to cultivate political backing for digital asset laws capable of enduring beyond President Donald Trump's administration.

Crypto political organizations have directed roughly $176 million toward over 100 midterm contests, with Fairshake and its affiliated committees responsible for approximately $150.8 million of that total expenditure. Industry groups are supporting candidates anticipated to champion long-term cryptocurrency legislation, while Ohio's Senate race has drawn more than $68 million in crypto-related election spending.

An investigation published by Reuters on October 27 reveals that campaign finance data shows Fairshake, along with its linked political action committees, utilized $150.8 million in spending by the October 20 mark, whereas additional crypto-aligned entities added approximately $25 million to election expenditures. This initiative aims at fielding candidates across both major political parties, as strategists within the sector characterize the move as a sustained strategy to cultivate ties with legislators capable of shaping upcoming cryptocurrency rules.

Fairshake stated that among the 58 individuals backed in primary and special contests, 53 secured victory. This super PAC entity, which draws main funding from Coinbase, Ripple, and Andreessen Horowitz, is currently directing its focus toward tight congressional battles, notably the Senate seat in Ohio.

Fairshake has emerged as the primary vehicle for political spending within the crypto sector, expanding upon its $196 million expenditure recorded during the 2024 election cycle. The organization operates through affiliated groups named Defend American Jobs and Protect Progress, which finance advertisements supporting or opposing candidates without direct coordination with their campaigns. These committees frequently run ads addressing topics such as taxes and immigration without explicitly discussing cryptocurrency, while spokesperson Geoff Vetter stated that the group supports candidates favoring American financial innovation across both parties.

According to a report dated October 5, every one of the 32 candidates backed the CLARITY Act designed to set federal regulations for digital asset markets. Earlier, the network declared its backing for 32 House incumbents, distributing an initial $6 million among three Democrats and three Republicans.

Industry representatives interviewed by Reuters stated that the group's political strategy extends beyond securing individual election victories, with Colin McLaren, head of government relations at the Solana Policy Institute, characterizing the spending as an investment in the future designed to secure predictable cryptocurrency regulations over the coming decades.

Regulatory shifts enacted under the Trump administration, notably the 2025 GENIUS Act which created a federal structure for payment stablecoins, have preceded this expenditure drive. Sector leaders contend that these laws provide enduring stability compared to regulatory approaches that subsequent governments might undo.

The industry's primary legislative focus remains the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, a bill designed to delineate responsibilities between the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. This legislation has encountered delays in the Senate due to disagreements regarding oversight mandates and specific clauses concerning public officials' cryptocurrency holdings.

While Fairshake maintains support for House candidates across party lines, Reuters reporting indicates that its Senate spending has leaned toward Republicans after Democrats opposed the clarity act. Consequently, the industry's political engagement is bipartisan in the House but follows a distinct pattern in Senate races. Significant funding for this initiative comes from Coinbase, Ripple, and Andreessen Horowitz, with a prior analysis estimating Ripple's election-related contributions at approximately $48 million during the 2026 cycle.

Ohio now stands as a top battleground for crypto-funded political activity, with entities funneling more than $68 million into the Senate matchup between Democrat Sherrod Brown and Republican Jon Husted, per advertising figures Reuters reviewed. Brown, who formerly led the Senate Banking Committee and opposed various industry-backed regulatory ideas before his 2024 defeat, has seen Fairshake-aligned groups pour resources into blocking his congressional comeback. His campaign has denounced these ads, some of which feature attacks unrelated to cryptocurrency rules. Reuters noted one ad recycling a false claim regarding IRS agents and Social Security benefits that fact-checkers had already debunked.

According to a report released on October 16, the group Defend American Jobs spent over $46 million opposing Sherrod Brown and backing Jon Husted, while a distinct political committee backed by the Winklevoss family disclosed nearly $6 million in associated expenditures within Ohio. Sherrod Brown's campaign has condemned these advertisements, noting that they feature attacks detached from cryptocurrency policy; Reuters found one ad reiterating a claim regarding Internal Revenue Service agents and Social Security benefits that had been previously rejected by the news agency's fact-checking unit.

Beyond Ohio, Fairshake has faced pushback from candidates claiming its ads unfairly targeted them. In Illinois, the network allocated over $10 million to oppose Lieutenant Governor Juliana Stratton and an additional $2.5 million against state Representative La Shawn Ford. Despite these efforts, both candidates won their primaries. Following the election, Ford's team sent a cease-and-desist letter alleging defamation tied to a federal indictment that officials had dismissed years prior.

Representative Ford connected the backlash to his backing of Illinois laws mandating registration for cryptocurrency firms with state regulators. Speaking about the ads aired on television, radio, and social platforms, he stated to Reuters that the effort felt personal and caused pain to his family.
