Curve Finance Sees Significant Loan Growth Amidst Sectoral Recovery

Presence Editorial · October 15, 2026 · 2 min read

In the third quarter of 2026, Curve Finance witnessed a substantial rise in its loan activity, with total active loans climbing to $70.9 million, marking an impressive 68% increase from the previous period. This growth was largely attributed to LlamaLend v2, which now accounts for about 84% of the net lending-debt increase during this quarter.

The sector-wide landscape also saw a downturn in Q3, with active loans totaling $32.8 billion by September, representing roughly 13% below the 2025 closing total. Despite these broader market challenges, Curve Finance's robust performance highlights its resilience and adaptability within the decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystem.

Quarterly Loan Growth and Market Share

According to the company, much of this growth can be attributed to LlamaLend v2, which has been expanding rapidly since June on Optimism and July on Ethereum mainnet. Borrowers using this platform can leverage their holdings as collateral while still retaining income generated by their assets, a feature that has attracted significant interest.

Looking ahead, Curve Finance anticipates further market launches of LlamaLend v2 in the fourth quarter, driven by ongoing demand from asset issuers. The company is also preparing for a vote on a proposed reUSD/sfrxUSD liquidity pool token market, which could significantly enhance its offerings and attract more users.

Future Plans and Regulatory Considerations

In related news, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has issued guidelines regarding the taxation of digital asset income, requiring taxpayers to maintain detailed records of their transactions. This regulatory development underscores the growing importance of compliance in the DeFi space, where Curve Finance is positioning itself as a leader.

While the sector faces challenges, Curve Finance's strong performance suggests that innovative solutions like LlamaLend v2 can drive growth even during times of broader market volatility.
