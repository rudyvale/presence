Russia Authorizes 30 Crypto Firms as EU Prepares Sanctions

Presence Editorial · November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

By Nov. 18, Russia's central bank listed 30 authorized crypto exchange operators on its public register, one day after new rules came into force permitting ordinary investors to purchase Bitcoin, Ether and Tether's USDT via regulated firms. Organized exchanges are eligible to commence trading on November 22, provided the central bank approves their governing rules.

The Moscow Exchange intends to initiate pilot trades on December 1 and will onboard participants as they become ready. EU diplomats informed Reuters that the bloc's upcoming sanctions package against Russia is anticipated to include restrictions on cryptocurrency. According to Kriptozakon's tally of the Bank of Russia's public register, there were 30 authorized crypto exchange operators in Russia as of November 17.

Regulatory Rollout and Authorized Operators

Kriptozakon's count of the Bank of Russia's public register indicates that 30 crypto exchange operators held authorization status in Russia by November 17. Sberbank and VTB feature on this roster, having joined the regulatory framework at separate times. Earlier reporting noted that VTB was one of the initial four entities registered in early October, while Sberbank first appeared as a digital custodian.

Interfax identified T-Invest Lab, Zefir, and Sistema Crypto as additional participants who entered the system early. The central bank's registry expanded throughout November. Data compiled from the Bank of Russia's filings by Kriptozakon reveals that Waybank, Derzhava Bank, and PlataPay received approval on November 13, with Tranomika following on November 16. These records mark the moment companies earned operational rights but do not specify when they began accepting customer orders.

VTB Deputy CEO Vitaly Sergeichuk stated in October that the bank anticipated launching trading via its VTB My Investments platform by November and through its dedicated crypto exchange by December, according to Interfax. Despite these early projections, as of November 17, both VTB and T-Bank were still in the process of preparing to offer retail crypto purchases.

The Bank of Russia's ordinance issued on November 17 designated BTC, ETH, and USDT as the specific currencies eligible for public trading admission. These rules apply to investors without qualified status, permitting them to purchase assets through brokers, trust managers, or crypto exchange offices with transactions allowed both on and off organized exchanges.

On November 17, the Bank of Russia released an ordinance identifying Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tether as the digital assets permitted for public trading. This framework extends to non-qualified investors, enabling them to acquire these assets via brokers, trust managers, or crypto exchange offices whether on or off organized markets. For residents without qualified status, the regulation imposes a yearly purchase ceiling of 300,000 rubles through a single broker; this limit aggregates the value of all cryptocurrencies purchased by that client, meaning holdings in both Bitcoin and Ether would be counted against the same threshold. Additionally, Moscow Exchange notes that retail participants must successfully complete an investor assessment prior to purchasing assets through a broker.

A distinct schedule governs organized exchanges, which may commence operations on November 22 following central bank approval of their trading protocols. The regulator mandates that these venues register entities including brokers, trust managers, and central counterparties while maintaining records of orders and trades and overseeing transaction flows. Furthermore, exchanges are required to submit trading activity reports to the central bank utilizing procedures consistent with those applied to other asset classes.

According to the Bank of Russia, a new regulatory timeline mandates that by July 1, 2027, individuals without authorization to manage digital currency dealings must either engage an approved operator or execute trades directly. This central bank instruction invokes Article 30 within the federal legislation governing digital currencies and digital rights.

Moscow Exchange announced in its Nov. 2 statement that it anticipates commencing operations with pilot deals and connecting participants as they become ready, listing Bitcoin, Ether, and USDT on the initial roster. Investors are expected to place orders through brokers, while the exchange plans to run the market from 6:50 a.m. to 11:50 p.m. Moscow time, aligning with its standard stock market hours.

Moscow Exchange intends to facilitate trades that settle either on the transaction date or the subsequent day, while also providing swaps for liquidity management. The exchange stated it will deploy the same trading technology used for its stock market and process crypto transactions via the National Clearing Centre.

Trading Timelines and Market Mechanics

Alexander Nam from HSE University's Center for Decentralized Finance and Digital Financial Assets Research told Cointelegraph that companies must still implement systems for investor testing, order execution, settlement, and custody.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stated that the Commission intends to unveil its next sanctions package against Russia prior to the Nov. 26–27 European Council summit, according to Reuters, with EU diplomats indicating the measures will target banks, cryptocurrencies, crypto platforms, and methods of circumventing sanctions via third countries. This upcoming action follows the bloc's 21st sanctions package adopted in October, which prohibited transactions involving 20 Russian banks and established a ban on dealings with RUBx, a ruble-linked cryptocurrency.
