House Oversight Committee Pushes for Ban on Official Prediction Market Trading

Presence Editorial · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

House Oversight Committee Chair James Comer is urging Congress to enact new restrictions on prediction market trading by government officials, a move potentially including an outright ban following the review of 1,700 pages of documents. The committee's investigation also incorporated five bipartisan staff briefings while considering whether to apply such trading limits across all branches of government.

According to a statement issued on November 13, the House Oversight Committee announced that its investigation looked into potential insider trading dangers posed by government officials, military members, and those holding security clearances. The proposed changes being discussed involve placing caps on contracts tied to the government and restricting data exchanges to support these new limits, which stands in opposition to current Senate regulations that prohibit its own members and employees from participating in prediction market trades.

Committee Demands Broad Restrictions on Federal Employees

Kentucky Republican Representative Comer stated that Congress ought to evaluate new legislation targeting federal employees, lawmakers, and congressional personnel regarding their participation in prediction markets. The proposed measures currently do not determine whether a total prohibition on official trading is necessary or if restrictions should instead focus only on specific contracts linked to government business operations.

Emphasizing the need for further reforms to curb public corruption and rebuild confidence in government institutions, Comer argued that limitations must extend across the executive, legislative, and judicial branches. The committee's findings assert that public servants are not permitted to derive personal financial gain from nonpublic information obtained through their official duties.

Lawmakers examined measures spanning total bans to tighter rules on state-linked agreements, pressing the legislative body to prompt firms and federal entities to exchange data required for compliance. These proposals define eligible traders and allowable contracts, signaling a push for statutory intervention as the panel outlines multiple strategies for legislators.

Operators Kalshi and Polymarket confirmed they tightened anti-insider mechanisms while probes reviewed protections to spot government staff on their sites. During the May 22 release, officials asked for documentation from Shayne Coplan of Polymarket and Tarek Mansour of Kalshi regarding identity checks, location limits, and unusual activity.

As of September 29, the House Oversight Committee had issued additional demands for information to Hyperliquid Labs, Crypto.com, and Aristotle Exchange, the entity behind PredictIt. The committee noted it had gathered almost 1,000 documents and five briefings from representatives of Kalshi and Polymarket. Its investigation sought to determine if individuals could circumvent limits on foreign-based sites; Comer remarked that accessing internal company files was necessary to pinpoint violators and evaluate if the operators fulfilled their statutory duties.

The probe referenced a prior instance concerning Army Master Sergeant Gannon Ken Van Dyke, whose charges claimed he leveraged classified details regarding an operation intended to apprehend former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro. He reportedly profited over $409,000 via bets on Polymarket before entering a not guilty plea. Authorities have previously intervened in matters involving data acquired through public service roles.

Former White House teleprompter operator Gabriel Perez reached a settlement with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission after regulators determined he utilized advance access to President Donald Trump's remarks for trading on Kalshi. The agency's announcement dated August 28 stated that Perez engaged in trades involving presidential mention contracts between December 2025 and February 2026, where payouts depended on whether the president used specific words or phrases during speeches.

In accordance with the settlement terms, Perez must repay $107,539.02 in earnings and remit a civil fine of $65,000. The agreement further restricts him from trading for three years, while the CFTC acknowledged KalshiEX's role in aiding their inquiry. Officials stated that the final penalty amount reflected a significant reduction permitted by cooperation guidelines, given Perez's participation in the probe.

In April, political candidates encountered distinct disciplinary measures from an exchange when Kalshi halted the accounts of three members of Congress—Mark Moran, Matt Klein, and Ezekiel Enriquez—following the discovery that they had traded agreements linked to their respective campaigns. The platform classified these actions as breaches of its internal regulations and confirmed that no referrals were made to either the CFTC or the Justice Department.

Investigation Targets Platforms and Offshore Loopholes

On April 30, the Senate implemented a prohibition on prediction markets through unanimous consent, instantly altering its governing rules. This measure was proposed by Republican Senator Bernie Moreno and applied to both senators and their personnel. Following this development, Republican Representative Ashley Hinson indicated her intention to propose an analogous resolution within the House.

Following the Senate's unanimous adoption of a prohibition on prediction markets on April 30, which instantly altered governing rules and applied to senators and their personnel under a resolution introduced by Republican Senator Bernie Moreno, Polymarket supported the resulting restrictions while stating its terms already prohibited the conduct. In response to this legislative shift, Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour confirmed that his company had proactively blocked members of Congress and enforced its insider trading rules. Subsequently, Republican Representative Ashley Hinson indicated her intention to propose an analogous resolution within the House.
