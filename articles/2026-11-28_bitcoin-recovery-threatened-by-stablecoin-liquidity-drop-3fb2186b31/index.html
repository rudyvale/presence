Bitcoin Recovery Threatened by Stablecoin Liquidity Drop

Presence Editorial · November 28, 2026 · 4 min read

Bitcoin has risen 2% to $61,719 even as the stablecoin market cap contracted by approximately 12% over the past 30 days, creating mounting liquidity pressure despite a short-term recovery in the broader cryptocurrency sector.

Ethereum gained 2.5% to reach $1,730.81 and Solana advanced 3.5% to trade at $73.48, while Tether and USDC maintain a combined market capitalization of approximately $243 billion; analysts note that CryptoQuant contributor CW_ reports signs of selling pressure being absorbed and 21Shares identifies Bitcoin's realized price of $53,250 as a key downside level.

Stablecoin Supply Contraction Limits Recovery Capital

In an analysis published on November 27, CryptoQuant contributor CryptoZeno cautioned that a contraction in stablecoin supply was diminishing the available capital needed to absorb selling pressure and sustain a Bitcoin recovery. At that time, data indicated Bitcoin was trading at $61,720, reflecting a 2% increase over the preceding 24 hours with daily volume hitting $23.17 billion against a market capitalization of $1.24 trillion.

Major altcoins posted comparable gains during this period as Ethereum climbed 2.5% to $1,730 and BNB rose 1.8% to $560. XRP advanced 2.6% to reach $0.98 while Solana gained 3.5% to trade at $73. CryptoZeno noted that stablecoins function as dollar-denominated liquidity essential for transactions across exchanges, trading pairs, and decentralized financial applications.

While market cap figures do not equate to immediate purchase intent, a growing supply injects additional funds that traders can utilize throughout the crypto ecosystem. In contrast, a shrinking supply diminishes the total capital available, forcing recovery efforts to rely more heavily on existing liquidity.

"Stablecoins fail to capture direct buying pressure," CryptoZeno stated. The expert pointed out that surges in stablecoin volume often aligned with Bitcoin's ascent during the 2024–2025 rally, yet this correlation has not remained steady, as shifts in supply have occasionally occurred before price changes in Bitcoin.

CryptoZeno outlined three prerequisites for a credible, lasting upward shift in market sentiment: a deceleration of the ongoing shrinkage in stablecoin reserves, a resumption of positive monthly increases in supply, and a strengthening of Bitcoin's price architecture. The researcher pointed out that short-term reductions in stablecoin volumes do not automatically trigger extended downturns, and the erratic data points from the start of 2023 highlight why single metrics should not be treated as conclusive indicators of market direction.

Before such scenarios materialize, the analyst cautioned that restricted liquidity might expose recovery efforts to further downturns. Even following the shrinkage noted by CryptoZeno, Tether (USDT) and USD Coin (USDC) still represent significant volumes of dollar-backed funds moving through the crypto sphere, keeping these instruments vital for system functions as total supply contracts.

As of November 27, the market cap for Tether stood at roughly $173.85 billion, with USD Coin adding another $69.15 billion to the total. Together, these two stablecoins generated $45.35 billion in daily trades, where Tether supplied $35.79 billion and USDC provided $9.55 billion. Even as worries about liquidity persisted, CW_ from CryptoQuant pointed out evidence suggesting that Bitcoin's recent sell-offs were being taken up by buyers.

During an evaluation on November 27, the analyst observed that metrics showing negative demand for Bitcoin had hovered at comparable levels for five straight days, yet prices did not worsen during that span. CW_ stated, "Someone is absorbing the selling $BTC, effectively offsetting the downward pressure," and added that if negative demand readings fell alongside a price uptick, this could set the stage for a stronger recovery.

Cryptocurrency asset manager 21Shares reported that the supply of stablecoins shrank by $10.7 billion across an 18-day span, constituting the most significant drop in dollar value since the Terra network failure in 2022. During this same interval, Bitcoin's demand signal stayed below zero; however, CW_ observed that the asset might endure selling pressure and establish a base for price gains should market conditions shift favorably.

Analysts Identify Conditions for a Sustained Bullish Reversal

Held stablecoin reserves on digital asset platforms dropped 15%, whereas American spot Bitcoin and Ethereum exchange-traded funds saw combined net outflows totaling $3.9 billion from October 30 through November 13. In prior reporting, investigators pinpointed Bitcoin's $53,250 realized price as a key support threshold, calculating this figure based on the average on-chain cost basis derived from the final transaction price for each coin to assess resilience during turbulent market shifts and possible capital exits.

Analysts stated that once Bitcoin slipped beneath its 200-week moving average close to $68,000, a rebound into the $73,000–$75,500 zone would be required to indicate robust upward momentum. They pointed to continued institutional capital entering via spot Bitcoin ETFs alongside increased stablecoin holdings on trading platforms as factors that might enable a more resilient market recovery.
