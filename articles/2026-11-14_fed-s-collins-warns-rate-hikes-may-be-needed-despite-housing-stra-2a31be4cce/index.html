Fed's Collins Warns Rate Hikes May Be Needed Despite Housing Strain

Presence Editorial · November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Susan Collins, who leads the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston, indicated that raising interest rates might occasionally be required to manage inflation, notwithstanding the fact that existing loan expenses are already diminishing housing affordability throughout the U.S. She contended that although stricter monetary measures could harm home building and mortgage demand, such actions are vital for ensuring long-term price stability.

Bitcoin dropped below $70,000 as investors assessed inflation concerns alongside the potential for additional Federal Reserve interest rate hikes. On November 11, US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw nearly $300 million in withdrawals, with BlackRock's IBIT fund accounting for the majority of those outflows.

Collins Balances Short-Term Pain with Long-Term Stability

Speaking during the Boston Fed's 70th Economic Conference on November 13, Collins noted that while stricter monetary rules may negatively impact home purchasing and building sectors, maintaining low inflation yields enduring economic advantages. She stated that elevated interest rates required to stabilize the economy and curb inflation will likely increase the cost of acquiring and constructing residences in the immediate future.

Collins further explained that consistent pricing fosters a more foreseeable economic landscape down the road, offering long-term support for housing. These remarks arrive as market participants anticipate another Federal Reserve rate increase in December, while Bitcoin and various digital assets face ongoing stress due to enduring inflationary pressures and declining institutional appetite.

Susan Collins, who leads the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston, noted that stricter monetary measures could eventually aid the housing sector. In her address, she highlighted the challenges confronting American families following multiple years of escalating property values and financing expenses. She explained that the sharp increase in home costs since the health crisis, alongside elevated mortgage interest rates beginning in early 2022, has made owning a home unattainable for numerous citizens.

Commercial entities have also faced repercussions. Collins observed that businesses throughout New England have encountered obstacles in hiring staff due to a lack of accessible or reasonably priced accommodation. She referenced instances where doctors, mechanics, researchers, and those working in hospitality left potential employment because they were unable to secure affordable residences.

Skilled labor shortages have caused certain housing projects to slow down or halt entirely. Collins noted that monetary policy exerts a particularly strong effect on the housing sector, as both construction spending and mortgage borrowing react sharply to changes in interest rates.

While stricter policies may restrict the availability of new homes and dampen demand for mortgages, she argued that managing inflation occasionally demands such actions. 'At times, tighter policy is required to secure price stability,' Collins stated, highlighting the enduring advantages this brings to housing and the broader economy. How might elevated interest rates impact Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies?

The remarks from Federal Reserve official Collins come as cryptocurrencies navigate a challenging environment where expectations of additional monetary tightening are dampening investor sentiment. On November 13, Bitcoin dropped below the $70,000 threshold, trading near $69,300 amid sustained selling pressure that also affected Ethereum, which slipped toward $1,950, and technology stocks.

The market pressure has intensified following the latest US inflation data, with Bitcoin falling below $74,000 on November 10 after consumer prices increased by 0.4% in October, surpassing economists' forecasts of 0.3%. Higher interest rates generally enhance the appeal of Treasury securities and other fixed-income investments, which can reduce demand for digital assets that lack comparable yield.

Excluding food and energy costs, core inflation climbed 0.3%, surpassing the predicted 0.2% level and causing market participants to rethink the Federal Reserve's stance on interest rate changes ahead of its December gathering. By November 10, betting markets had placed an 85% chance on a quarter-point hike, with a further 12.7% wagered on a more substantial adjustment.

Crypto Markets React to Inflation Data and Rate Expectations

Deutsche Bank now shares this wary perspective, predicting three successive quarter-point increases in the federal funds rate for December, January, and March, which would elevate the target range to 4.50%–4.75%. Concurrently, US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds saw $299.9 million leave the market on November 11, with BlackRock's IBIT fund responsible for $269.73 million of that reduction.

On November 11, net outflows from U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds reached $299.9 million, a figure dominated by BlackRock's IBIT product which saw a decline of $269.73 million. Ethereum-based ETFs also recorded losses of $144.81 million that day, contributing to cumulative outflows of approximately $1.44 billion over five straight trading days. Market attention now turns to the Federal Reserve's upcoming rate announcement on December 9, preceded by the release of October policy meeting minutes on November 18 and the personal consumption expenditures inflation data on November 25.
