Binance Restarts Withdrawals Following $6.34 Billion Hack

Presence Editorial · November 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Binance has resumed withdrawal operations across nearly all networks following the completion of a phased recovery process that started on Nov. 1 after its $6.34 billion October hack, though a small number of chains remain paused for routine maintenance or planned upgrades. The exchange reported recording $3.64 billion in net outflows during the first nine and a half hours of this reopening on Nov. 1, stating that it covered the affected assets with company funds while publishing a reserve snapshot covering 198 tokens.

According to Onchain Lens data from November 26, an additional $35.52 million in Ether moved out of wallets associated with the breach, a moment when Binance declared that efforts to reinstate withdrawals following the October security event had finished. The platform also stated on that day that only a minimal portion of standard withdrawal activity was impacted by other networks still experiencing maintenance or planned upgrades.

Phased Recovery and Network Status

Binance advised customers to verify network status pages for specific assets when processing withdrawals, noting that delays may stem from the blockchain itself rather than the exchange. The platform's notice issued on Nov. 26 clarified that deposit and withdrawal statuses are listed separately, directing users to check both the asset and the network they intend to use before proceeding with transactions.

Following the Oct. 29 breach, Binance suspended withdrawals but initiated their restoration on Nov. 1, with Bitcoin withdrawals resuming first among the selected assets. The exchange's initial recovery schedule covered 15 networks, including Ether and stablecoins such as USDT and USDC on Ethereum, while deposits remained open throughout the suspension period.

Binance stated that it prioritized the first group of tokens based on customer demand observed in the preceding week, executing security and stability checks before restoring each asset and network pair. In its update dated Nov. 14, the exchange reported having reopened withdrawals on 87 networks during that week, which included Cardano, Litecoin, and Avalanche, while targeting the completion of the remaining networks and assets by the end of the following week. The final announcement regarding this phase arrived 25 days after the first withdrawals resumed.

The initial reopening triggered a surge in activity as Binance processed $4.65 billion in withdrawals and $1.01 billion in deposits between 06:00 and 15:30 UTC on Nov. 1, resulting in net outflows of $3.64 billion for that period. The exchange noted that withdrawal volumes subsequently eased following this initial spike.

Binance stated that it had completed covering all assets impacted by the security incident before lifting the final withdrawal restrictions, committing more than $6.34 billion in company funds to purchase affected tokens on the market and through over-the-counter desks.

Binance utilized funds from its corporate treasury, alongside the Secure Asset Fund for Users and a segment of its futures insurance fund. The platform indicated that the amount taken from the futures fund exceeded the minimum threshold mandated by its risk-management framework. Additionally, the company promised to restore the user protection fund to approximately $1 billion.

Binance stated that a reserve snapshot captured at 00:00 UTC on Nov. 12 encompassed 198 tokens, with the accompanying cryptographic proof generated by the exchange on Nov. 14 to verify that user assets were backed at least 1:1. The platform indicated it would manage the small number of affected tokens it no longer supports via its delisting process and cover customers who still hold them.

Earlier on Nov. 26, blockchain tracker Onchain Lens reported $35.52 million in transfers involving Ether held in wallets linked to the hack, while estimating remaining unfrozen holdings at around $1.9 billion. This total included 18,350 Bitcoin worth $1.11 billion and roughly 462,000 Ether worth $785.2 million, with an additional estimated $2.6 billion held in frozen stablecoins.

Funding Sources and Reserve Proof

Three days prior to these reports, the U.S. Justice Department initiated a civil forfeiture action seeking more than $1.1 billion in Circle-issued USDC that prosecutors allege originated from the theft. The department stated that these stablecoins were already subject to a court restraining order and noted that U.S. authorities had assisted in freezing, seizing, or returning nearly $3 billion in cryptocurrency from multiple sources since the breach occurred.

In a federal court proceeding in Washington, D.C., authorities have submitted a forfeiture complaint targeting USDC tokens linked to 36 distinct addresses spanning 18 different blockchains. This civil action uniquely lists the cryptocurrency as the accused party.
