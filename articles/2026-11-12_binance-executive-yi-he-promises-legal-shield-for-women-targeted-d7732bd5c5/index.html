Binance Executive Yi He Promises Legal Shield for Women Targeted by Sexual Rumors

Presence Editorial · November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Binance co-CEO Yi He has denounced false sexual allegations aimed at women within the crypto sector and committed to offering legal assistance to female staff members impacted by these incidents. The firm's legal team intends to safeguard employees who are the subject of invented sexual stories. In her remarks, Yi He noted that such assaults have occurred against women associated with competing exchanges, BNB initiatives, and industry collaborators. This declaration did not reveal plans for litigation, name specific accused parties, or define the applicable legal venue.

In a message posted to X on Nov. 11, Yi He stated that female professionals throughout the field have been ensnared in manufactured controversies, such as cases where staff images were shared with these accusations. She outlined a trend where women acting as public faces for organizations become focal points of rivalry. Her rebuke included incidents directed at Binance personnel, individuals engaged in BNB ecosystem endeavors, influencers linked to the platform, and female workers at rival firms.

Executive Pledges Legal Support Amidst Industry-Wide Rumor Campaigns

On Nov. 11, Yi He announced that Binance's legal team would offer protection to female staff members caught up in recent disputes and extended an invitation for women to enter a workplace characterized by fairness and transparency. This assurance appeared in an English version posted on X, where she confirmed the legal unit's readiness to back those employees.

According to Yi He, her remarks followed two days of internet chatter that subjected women to sexualized conjecture. She pointed to prior cases where Chinese media circulated allegations she blamed on a rival firm alongside images of female workers, labeling such actions as recurring assaults against women holding public positions.

Yi He, a leader at Binance, confirmed that harassment efforts against personnel had endured and expanded past the company's boundaries. She recounted how she herself encountered false allegations following the theft and improper use of her identity documents. Although she claimed resilience against negative commentary, Yi He voiced worry regarding the impact on the relatives and acquaintances of staff when such content surfaced frequently in news outlets. She noted that the female employees remained engaged in resolving client issues despite their personal anguish.

A response posted by the authenticated Misa_OKX profile expressed gratitude for her advocacy on behalf of women. In another message within the same discussion thread, Yi He challenged the intentions driving these assaults, urging observers to consider the potential benefits an initiator might seek from such conduct.

Binance executive Yi He linked her critique of workplace inequality to the extra time and effort required for women to reach leadership roles, noting that professional achievements must be evaluated independently of gender while describing harassment as a prevalent issue. She highlighted how assumptions regarding parental leave and career commitment often influence hiring decisions, criticizing an attitude where some women view marriage as an exit strategy when employment becomes unsatisfying.

Yi He further argued that these societal expectations lead managers to favor male candidates, reinforcing the need for equal treatment based solely on professional results. While defending employees facing rumors, she emphasized that her ability to withstand personal criticism does not negate the impact such media exposure has on their families and friends, observing that the affected women continued managing customer responsibilities without seeking her intervention despite their distress.

Beyond the specific case, numerous female professionals have openly addressed various types of discrimination. A November 2023 conversation with the report highlighted Gracy Chen's struggles with gender-based obstacles during capital raising efforts. At that time serving as Bitget's managing director, Chen noted she faced both implicit and explicit bias while seeking investment for her tech venture.

Mary Pedler, a founder in communications who spoke to the same piece, revealed that female staff comprised 80 percent of her workforce. She suggested establishing internal groups with decision-making power regarding recruitment and advancement, complemented by programs for mentoring and developing leadership skills.

Critique of Workplace Bias and Hiring Assumptions in Crypto Sector

Issues regarding female involvement persisted throughout the current year. During an April gathering at EthCC, Sarah Akwisombe, who specializes in growth and community affairs, connected the drop in attendance to reductions in events, marketing, and public relations functions, noting these areas employ many women. Her remarks focused on employment reductions and conference presence instead of the sexual allegations attributed to Yi He.

An assessment conducted in March 2025 regarding disparities in gender and compensation referenced a Pantera Capital study from 2024 that polled 502 individuals across the United States. The data indicated that female participants earned a median salary 14.67% above that of males. Pantera attributed this result partially to professional tenure, observing that women in the group generally held jobs needing a minimum of five years experience, whereas entry-level positions were filled more frequently by men.
