Milei Attacks Five Journalists Following LIBRA Case Dismissal

Presence Editorial · October 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Following a judicial decision to drop criminal charges involving Javier Milei, his sister Karina, and nine additional defendants in the LIBRA matter, Argentine President Javier Milei identified five journalists as targets of his criticism regarding their reporting on the case. He further questioned who would make amends for what he claims constitutes harm to his public standing.

Hugo Alconada Mon, a journalist, noted that certain investigative steps were unfinished prior to the court's decision. This development occurred after a distinct U.S. class-action lawsuit concluded in September. La Nación stated that Milei posted comments on X on October 23, charging that journalists and political rivals are exploiting the probe to weaken his administration.

President Demands Compensation for Alleged Reputation Damage

Argentine President Javier Milei issued a statement on October 23 via the platform X, characterizing the period since the LIBRA token's launch as a year, eight months and nine days filled with alleged lies, slander, and insults. In this English translation of his Spanish remarks, he framed the recent court dismissal as vindication while accusing his critics of pursuing political objectives through legal channels. He specifically asked who would compensate him now, according to a newspaper reproduction of the text.

The statement reproduced by La Nación explicitly named Hugo Alconada Mon, Carlos Pagni, Paulino Rodríguez, Natalia Volosín, and Luis Novaresio as targets for spreading falsehoods and conducting operations against his government. Milei alleged that Grupo La Nación's coverage was connected to an effort to pressure his administration regarding taxes, though the reviewed material does not independently establish this specific allegation. This action occurred immediately after a ruling that encompassed the president, his sister Karina, and nine other defendants in the LIBRA criminal case.

While commending the courts for standing firm against influence from both political and media sectors, he directed his sharp remarks at the news industry. La Nación published his assertion that the probe aimed to harm the presidency under the guise of addressing coup-related motives.

During a distinct gathering organized by Fundación Faro, an institution directed by Agustín Laje, Milei restated his request for financial restitution and claimed that institutional mechanisms were being used to oust him. Infobae noted the presence of U.S. Ambassador Peter Lamelas, Argentina's envoy to the United States Alec Oxenford, alongside government officials and business executives at the function.

According to iProfesional, the presidential office released a declaration praising the verdict. This body characterized the legal process as a maneuver intended to tarnish Milei's standing ahead of the election while vouching for the judiciary's behavior.

According to La Nación, the presidential office invoked Article 336, subsection 3, of Argentina's criminal procedure code, a provision that mandates dismissal when the alleged actions fail to meet the definition of a crime. In a distinct social media post also shared by La Nación, Karina Milei expressed approval of the result, noting that the prior charges had harmed the siblings' standing and framing the ruling as evidence that reality had ultimately won.

Judge Marcelo Martínez de Giorgi ended the prosecution on Oct. 23, a ruling made after prosecutor Eduardo Taiano asked for dismissal and without summoning any defendant for formal questioning.

The underlying matter involved the February 2025 launch and subsequent collapse of LIBRA, a Solana token that President Milei promoted as a private initiative to support Argentine businesses, details he shared in a launch-day post including the project's website and contract address. Among those targeted, journalist Alconada Mon wrote for La Nación regarding the dismissal, reporting that certain evidence-gathering measures remained incomplete and noting that affected investors had been removed from their status as private complainants.

According to the account, the judge determined that while the president's prominence might have enhanced the token's profile and fostered trust, these factors did not constitute criminal fraud. Alconada Mon noted in his reporting that certain blockchain tracking efforts remained unfinished due to a shortage of authorized technical equipment. He also stated that replies from the firms under investigation were incomplete.

The newspaper conveyed that Pedro Biscay, who leads the Center for Research and Prevention of Economic Crime, deemed the dismissal too early and urged a comprehensive examination. The group served as an amicus curiae throughout the legal process.

Prior to Javier Milei's recent remarks, the LIBRA class action in the United States had been terminated, as coverage from October 2 described how Judge Jennifer L. Rochon rejected assertions against Meteora, former CEO Benjamin Chow, and the Kelsier defendants on September 29. Her ruling approved motions to dismiss while refusing leave for a second amended complaint, instructing the clerk to finalize the docket.

Incomplete Investigation and Technical Limitations Cited by Reporters

The judge's order applied distinct legal bases for ending each claim: counts alleging fraud or conspiracy against the Kelsier defendants did not state a valid claim, whereas other charges were dropped due to insufficient personal jurisdiction. Similarly, assertions directed at Chow and Meteora were dismissed because they failed to state a claim, highlighting separate legal shortcomings instead of one uniform procedural result.

In August 2025, prior to the September ruling, Judge Jennifer L. Rochon removed a hold on $57.6 million in USDC associated with wallets connected to Davis and Chow. At that juncture, reports indicated investors were pursuing damages exceeding $100 million. The opinion issued in September names Omar Hurlock from Brooklyn, New York, and Anuj Mehta from Arlington, Virginia as the plaintiffs. Each filed the suit representing their own interests alongside those of other investors.
