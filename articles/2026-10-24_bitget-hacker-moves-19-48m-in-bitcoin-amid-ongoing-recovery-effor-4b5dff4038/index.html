Bitget Hacker Moves $19.48M in Bitcoin Amid Ongoing Recovery Efforts

Presence Editorial · October 24, 2026 · 5 min read

According to Arkham, the Bitget intruder has executed fifty-two transactions totaling $19.48 million, a sum composed almost exclusively of Bitcoin while monitoring recent withdrawals from wallets associated with the September security incident. Of this aggregate amount, $19.33 million was specifically attributed to Bitcoin holdings.

Between October 15 and October 23, funds linked to the hacker were dispatched to new destinations in a value of $78.01 million. Arkham estimates that remaining labeled assets sit at $242.54 million, primarily in Bitcoin, though the final resting places of these recent movements are unknown, suggesting possible laundering remains unverified. The blockchain analysis firm timestamped these specific transfers from 10:02 UTC on October 21 through 02:48 UTC on October 24, noting that Bitcoin represented $19.33 million while Ethereum comprised the balance; their commentary indicated nearly all capital flowed to previously unseen addresses.

Arkham Tracks Fresh Outflows to Unidentified Addresses

Blockchain analytics provider Arkham presented its latest commentary on the Bitget situation as an inquiry instead of a confirmed fact. The company's communications deliberately refrain from identifying any particular exchange, mixing service, or peer-to-peer counterparty that accepted the most recent movements, observing merely that money exited designated wallets while failing to determine if those holdings were liquidated, converted to fiat, or reclaimed.

Across a broader reporting window, Arkham stated that the attacker had sent $78.01 million to new addresses since October 15. A daily breakdown of this activity showed $23.31 million moving on October 15, followed by $8.68 million on October 16 and $1.15 million on October 17.

According to Arkham's analysis covering a 64-hour and 46-minute window, another $10.51 million was transferred on October 18, followed by subsequent movements of $15.13 million on October 20, $11.74 million on October 21, $184,160 on October 22, and $7.3 million on October 23 after a period of inactivity on October 19. A later post within the same thread described this aggregate activity as occurring over "the past 48 hours," creating a discrepancy between that shorthand description and the precise timestamps provided for each transaction.

Based on rounded asset figures, Bitcoin comprised roughly 99.2% of the $19.48 million total, suggesting an Ethereum portion near $150,000, even though Arkham omitted a precise token count in its first update. The company assessed the hacker's remaining labeled assets at $242.54 million, a sum containing 2,960.01 BTC valued at $241.99 million plus minor holdings across Ethereum, Hypercore, Solana, and Tron. Arkham's updates do not identify an exchange, mixer, or over-the-counter desk accepting the recent transfers, recording funds exiting labeled wallets while failing to confirm if those assets were liquidated, converted to cash, or reclaimed.

Bitget has revised its accounting for the security incident to approximately $387.5 million, which represents a 69% portion of that total balance according to the firm's description. This adjustment incorporates Zcash and TRON assets that were omitted from an initial calculation of roughly $351.6 million, a change attributed to a more complete classification of original transfers rather than additional theft. The exchange previously stated a lower figure of $350 million in related contexts, but the updated accounting reflects the inclusion of these specific digital asset classes in the total loss assessment.

Arkham's data snapshot values the hacker's remaining labeled holdings at $242.54 million, which equals approximately 62.6% of Bitget's revised $387.5 million estimate. This comparison does not establish how much has been recovered, as the figures contrast remaining labeled holdings with dollar estimates from the original incident rather than tracking recovery progress. Arkham records $239.34 million in these holdings, including approximately 2,958 BTC valued at $238.79 million, while also noting smaller balances across Ethereum, Hypercore, Solana, and Tron.

Prior to October, activity connected to the breach started, with AMLBot identifying about four BTC moved into CoinJoin during September in a move seemingly designed to hide illicit money. The entity tracked holdings originating on TRON, converting them to USDT, then shifting across to Ethereum via USDT0. From there, ETH swaps and THORChain facilitated movement until the assets arrived at Bitcoin, where roughly 4.59 BTC appeared from that specific path before being divided into lesser quantities.

Separate analysis by Bitquery recorded additional September conversions utilizing THORChain and Chainflip. This work aligned 66 swaps with payments totaling 126.71 BTC as of 14:38 UTC on Sep. 25, noting two swaps remained pending at that moment. These earlier pathways fail to confirm where Arkham's most recent transfers went, since the October images display transaction graphs yet lack legible evidence linking fresh outflows to those same platforms.

Bitget's incident explainer, updated on October 20, states that its phased withdrawal restoration concluded on October 2 following the reopening of Bitcoin, Ethereum, and USDT services in September. The company asserts that customer account balances remain unaffected and reports no further unauthorized transfers have occurred since containment measures were implemented.

According to the same explainer, Bitget pointed to a flaw in an external security tool as the cause of the breach. The company explained that the intruder secured elevated internal access and issued fake withdrawal orders that circumvented current risk measures, adding that the compromised systems were quarantined and the issue has been resolved.

Bitget CEO Gracy Chen confirmed earlier reports regarding the reopening of withdrawals and noted that the Protection Fund exceeded $300 million as of September 30, with the company stating this fund is designated to absorb financial losses from the breach.

Discrepancies in Reported Transfer Timelines and Asset Values

A notice issued on September 25 identified Mandiant and SlowMist as investigation partners and listed receiving addresses across Ethereum-compatible networks, the XRP Ledger, Zcash, and TRON, while indicating that some assets had been frozen through industry coordination without specifying an aggregate frozen amount in that document.

Bitget's recovery initiative grants distinct 5% incentives for qualifying voluntary steps that directly immobilize or retrieve impacted holdings, while clearly omitting deeds executed pursuant to judicial mandates, police directives, or additional legal frameworks and keeping judgment regarding qualification and disbursement.
