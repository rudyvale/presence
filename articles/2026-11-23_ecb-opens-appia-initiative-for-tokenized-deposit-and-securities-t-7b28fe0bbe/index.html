ECB Opens Appia Initiative for Tokenized Deposit and Securities Trials

Presence Editorial · November 23, 2026 · 4 min read

The European Central Bank is calling on financial market collectives to evaluate tokenized deposits and securities within the Appia framework, requiring initial applications by Dec. 11.

To proceed, applicants need to form a coalition of separate legal bodies and outline their collaborative methods. These deposit trials will employ simulated central bank funds to examine payment settlement mechanisms for banks. Chosen teams should deliver results in 2027, preceding the Appia blueprint scheduled for 2028.

Strict Eligibility Rules for Deposit Workstreams

The ECB invitation, published on Nov. 20, grants groups until noon Central European Time on Dec. 11, 2026, to submit applications for the first round of trials, with later reviews conducted on a rolling basis. Eligible entities may apply to participate in one or more of three workstreams that cover bank deposits and payments, securities, and the regulations governing shared networks.

According to ECB eligibility criteria, entities concentrating on deposits or shared network governance must include a minimum of five independent legal bodies located in no fewer than two euro-area nations. Most of these qualifying entities need to be financial institutions that are authorized, supervised, or overseen by regulators. Furthermore, at least two participants must either engage with TARGET, the Eurosystem's settlement system, or demonstrate eligibility for access.

The securities workstream grants applicants greater flexibility by allowing smaller groups to qualify if they fulfill the necessary roles, specifically requiring two market participants alongside a ledger operator eligible to connect to Pontes. This stream is subject to a one-country euro-area threshold and mandates that proposals demonstrate a credible path to settlement in central bank money.

Applicants may submit their proposals via the online form linked from the ECB's notice or request an offline version by email if they encounter difficulties with the digital interface. The Eurosystem will evaluate submissions based on technical readiness, committed staff and resources, expected deliverables, and the diversity of countries and participant types represented within the group.

Each participating group is required to designate two experts drawn from different member institutions alongside a separate contact person to liaise with the Eurosystem. Selected participants must execute a written agreement and assume responsibility for all associated costs, while also providing the necessary hosting and technical support for their respective test environments.

According to the annex, the deposit workstream will look into methods for banks to net mutual payments, resolve arising obligations, and finalize settlements via a trial iteration of tokenized central bank funds. The document notes that participating groups are permitted to employ their own distributed ledgers for these trials while also seeking cooperation on shared business protocols and connection techniques when deposits are issued across varied networks.

In the United Kingdom, participating banks in a tokenized deposit trial simulated a marketplace purchase by reserving buyer funds until delivery confirmation. The group intends to test these deposits within digital bond settlements during early 2027. Separately, Cosmos Chief Commercial Officer Eran Barak stated in September that its bank tokenization network could link separate ledgers, though banks must still agree on liquidity practices and the timing when payments become final.

The European Central Bank requests that groups proposing shared network governance provide a specific example, such as tokenized payments, to illustrate necessary network rules. Its annex identifies access controls, compliance measures, and connections with existing financial infrastructure as areas where groups could demonstrate capabilities, while formal standards work is scheduled for a separate call.

The securities workstream will examine how firms might transfer collateral within repurchase agreements and recycle cash across connected transactions to reduce the amount of funds held in advance. The European Central Bank is soliciting proposals regarding settlement mechanisms and programmable attributes for instruments like tokenized money market funds, specifying what corporate treasurers require to utilize them. Groups addressing more than one focus area must submit a distinct proposal for each area.

Lynq CEO Jerald David explained that separate settlement systems can compel firms to park funds at multiple trading venues or with various counterparties beforehand. He noted that while a firm may possess sufficient capital, it can still face difficulties moving those funds to the party requiring them in a timely manner.

Flexible Pathways for Securities and Network Governance

In a Frankfurt address delivered on November 20, Christine Lagarde, President of the ECB, cautioned that if platforms implement conflicting regulations, they risk severing their capacity to execute trades with each other. She emphasized that shared standards are essential and must be established by lawmakers. The Eurosystem anticipates that activities planned for 2027 will shape its Appia blueprint, a document scheduled for release in 2028 intended to outline conclusions and suggestions regarding Europe's tokenized financial sectors.

Since its September debut, Pontes has enabled institutions to finalize wholesale deals using central bank funds. The European Stability Mechanism utilized this tool to complete the settlement of its inaugural digitally native bond, a issuance totaling €1 billion. In that transaction, Clearstream managed the transfer of securities via its digital infrastructure, whereas the cash component cleared through Pontes within the Eurosystem's current high-value payment network.
